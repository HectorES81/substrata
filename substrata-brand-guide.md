# SUBSTRATA — Brand Identity System
### substrata.be — v2.0

---

## A note on language

This guide uses words like *rigorous*, *evidence-informed*, and *theoretically grounded* rather than *scientific*. That distinction is intentional. Substrata is built on validated academic frameworks — the Big Five, ECR-R, Gottman, Schwartz, Haidt, MSCEIT — but the platform's own instrument has not been independently validated against a normative sample. Calling it scientific would be borrowing credibility we haven't yet earned. What we can honestly claim is more interesting: a structured, theoretically grounded profiling system that becomes more accurate as more evidence accumulates. That is a defensible position. It is also a better brand story.

## 01 — Brand positioning statement

Substrata maps the personality beneath the personality — a structured, evidence-informed profiling instrument, built on the most rigorous frameworks available, that reveals what you are made of gradually and honestly, with increasing confidence the more of yourself you give it.

---

## 02 — Core brand pillars

| Pillar | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Depth over surface** | We map structure, not presentation. Users learn things about themselves they didn't know. |
| **Evidence over assertion** | Every claim comes with a confidence level. We don't present partial data as complete. |
| **Honest over flattering** | Results reflect what the data shows, not what users want to hear. |
| **Patient over instant** | The profile develops across sessions. This is a feature, not a limitation. |
| **Revealing, not defining** | We surface patterns. We do not assign identities. A profile is a model, not a verdict. |
| **Grounded over mystical** | Theoretical foundations are named and real. We don't dress up speculation as insight. |

## 05 — Typography system

### Display font: Playfair Display
Signals accumulated knowledge, tradition, and rigor. Used for all primary headlines and brand statements. The serif choice is deliberate — it reads like a publication, not an app.

- H1: Playfair Display / 400 / 40–56px / tracking −0.02em
- H2: Playfair Display / 400 / 26–32px / tracking −0.01em
- Pull quotes / brand statements: Playfair Display / 400 Italic / 18–22px

### Body font: DM Sans
Clean, geometric, precise. No personality of its own — it gets out of the way of the content.

- H3 / subsection labels: DM Sans / 500 / 13–15px / tracking +0.04em / uppercase
- Body: DM Sans / 400 / 15–16px / line-height 1.7
- Caption / metadata: DM Sans / 400 / 12px / line-height 1.5 / Slate color
- Confidence labels: DM Sans / 500 / 12px / uppercase / tracking +0.06em

### Hierarchy rules

1. H1 is always serif. Never DM Sans at display size.
2. H3 and below are always sans-serif. Never Playfair Display at small sizes.
3. Data and scores are always DM Sans — numbers in a serif feel decorative. Numbers in a sans feel measured.
4. Confidence percentages and session counts use DM Sans / 500 with a Slate or Ochre color — never Terracotta, which is reserved for actions and primary emphasis.
5. Italic serif is used only for brand statements, pull quotes, and taglines — not for UI copy.

## 06 — Visual system

### Primary metaphor: geological cross-section

The brand's core visual language is the cross-section — layers made visible, structure revealed. This appears in:

- The logo mark (if developed): a simplified stratum diagram or core sample
- Data visualization: personality dimensions shown as geological layers, not radar charts or bar graphs
- Onboarding graphics: depth indicators, layers gradually appearing as sessions accumulate
- Marketing: photography of actual geological cross-sections, rock cores, sediment samples

### Secondary metaphor: scientific mapping and field notation

A second visual register adds measurement language — the instruments of a careful researcher rather than a test-taker. This includes:

- Contour maps and survey grids
- Confidence bands (ranges, not point estimates)
- Calibration marks and annotation systems
- Field notebook aesthetics: gridded paper, pencil annotations, measurement notation
- The visual grammar of a geological survey, not a personality quiz

### What this looks like together

Think: a natural history museum that was redesigned by a minimalist studio. Or: a field researcher's notebook published by a design-forward press. The reference is rigorous and human simultaneously — instruments, paper, earth tones, careful notation.

### What this explicitly avoids

- Radar/spider charts (associated with game stats and corporate performance reviews)
- Progress bars with celebratory completion states
- Pulse animations, glow effects, anything that implies excitement
- Clean white tech product UI (wrong category signal)
- Organic blob shapes, watercolor washes, hand-drawn illustration (wellness signal)

## 07 — Voice and tone

### The voice in one sentence

Calm, evidence-minded, slightly literary. Speaks like a thoughtful person who has done the reading and doesn't need to prove it.

### Tone parameters

- Confident, not authoritative. The brand has a point of view; it doesn't lecture.
- Precise, not clinical. Uses specific language because vague language is less honest.
- Warm, not soft. Rigorous and human are not opposites.
- Dry wit is permitted. Inspirational register is not.
- Intellectual humility is structural, not performative. We state confidence levels because partial data presented as complete is dishonest — not to seem humble.

### The useful shorthand

Like a favorite professor, not a dissertation. Rigorous in what it claims; clear in how it says it.

### Do / Don't examples

**On delivering results:**
- Do: *"Your Conscientiousness score is lower than your self-report suggested — not a flaw, just a more accurate data point than your self-image."*
- Don't: *"Don't worry — every score is valid! You're amazing just as you are."*

**On progress:**
- Do: *"Three sessions in, your Openness and Agreeableness dimensions are at high confidence. Your Emotional Stability picture is still developing — we need more data before we can say anything useful."*
- Don't: *"You're on a journey of self-discovery! Keep unlocking your potential."*

**On compatibility:**
- Do: *"Compatible isn't the same as easy. The profiles that sustain each other over time often look like productive friction."*
- Don't: *"Find your soulmate! Our algorithm matches you with the love you deserve."*

**On uncertainty:**
- Do: *"This pattern appears consistently across four sessions. It may be stable; it may shift as more evidence accumulates."*
- Don't: *"This is who you are."*

**On the product itself:**
- Do: *"Substrata is built on the most robust personality frameworks available. Whether our own instrument achieves the same standard is something we're working to establish — and we'll tell you the confidence level as we do."*
- Don't: *"Our revolutionary AI-powered platform uses cutting-edge science to unlock your true self."*

## 08 — The confidence mechanic — brand language

The confidence score is not just a product feature. It is the brand's clearest expression of intellectual honesty and its most defensible differentiator. Use these framing principles consistently:

- A profile is not a verdict. It is a model that improves as evidence accumulates.
- Confidence grows as more layers are mapped.
- Low confidence is not a failure state — it is honest about what we don't yet know.
- Never show a score without its confidence level. Presenting partial data as complete is the exact problem Substrata exists to solve.
- The instrument itself is subject to the same standard: we are building confidence in our own methodology over time, and we say so.

### Confidence language examples

- "Your Openness dimension is at 84% confidence across six sessions."
- "Attachment pattern: emerging. Two sessions is not enough to say anything reliable here."
- "This is one of our higher-confidence findings. It has appeared consistently since session two."
- "We're still mapping this. Check back after your next session."

## 09 — UI microcopy

**Button / primary CTA**
> Begin mapping.

**Empty state — profile not yet complete**
> Your profile is still forming. At 60% confidence, we'll surface compatible people. You're at 38%. Come back after your next session.

**Onboarding — session one intro**
> This will take about twenty minutes. There are no correct answers. The instrument doesn't know what you want to hear, and it won't try to guess.

**Progress message — after session three**
> Three sessions in. Your Openness and Agreeableness dimensions are now at high confidence. Your Emotional Stability picture is still developing — we need more data before we can say anything useful.

**Notification — new insight available**
> Something shifted. Your attachment pattern looks different from session one. It may be growth, or it may be that we're getting a cleaner signal. Either way, worth reading.

**Low confidence warning**
> This finding is based on two sessions. Treat it as a preliminary signal, not a conclusion.

## 10 — Brand dos and don'ts

### Do

1. Use geological and mapping metaphors — layers, strata, cross-section, depth, core sample, survey, confidence band
2. Write in complete sentences. The brand does not fragment into bullet points.
3. State confidence levels alongside every significant claim, in product and in copy
4. Name the theoretical frameworks when relevant (Big Five, ECR-R, Gottman) — this is what "theoretically grounded" looks like
5. Acknowledge what the instrument doesn't yet know
6. Let silence do work — whitespace is intelligence, not waste
7. Frame connection as a consequence of self-knowledge, not the goal
8. Treat warmth as structural (good writing, human framing) not cosmetic (soft words, exclamation points)
9. Use dry wit sparingly and without announcing it
10. Apply the same epistemic standards to the brand that the product applies to users

### Don't

1. Use the word "scientific" to describe Substrata's own instrument until it has been independently validated
2. Use "journey," "authentic self," "deserve," "unlock," "soulmate," or "revolutionary"
3. Use emoji anywhere in the product interface or marketing copy
4. Use pastels, gradients, or any visual language associated with wellness or mental health brands
5. Use red, bright blue, or high-contrast urgency combinations
6. Make promises about outcomes — finding love, improving relationships, becoming a better person
7. Use exclamation points, except in genuine emergencies
8. Reference MBTI, Enneagram, or other frameworks dismissively — position away from them, not against them
9. Show scores without confidence levels
10. Design for speed or virality — the experience is slow by design and should feel that way

## 11 — Brand story — about page opening

Most platforms ask you to present yourself. Substrata asks you to be examined. There is a difference: one produces a photograph, the other produces a core sample.

We built this because the frameworks that best explain human personality — the Big Five, Attachment Theory, Schwartz Values, Gottman's relationship research — are precise, empirically grounded, and almost entirely absent from the technology that claims to help people connect. We wanted to build something that took those frameworks seriously.

What we have built is not a scientific instrument in the strict sense. It is a structured profiling system informed by validated research — and we are honest about that distinction. What it does that most personality products don't is acknowledge uncertainty. Your profile has a confidence level. It grows as more evidence accumulates. It may revise. This is not a flaw in the system; it is the system working correctly.

The name comes from geology: the layer beneath the visible surface, where the actual structure of a thing is found. A stratum is not discovered quickly. It takes excavation, time, and instruments calibrated for the work.

That is what we are trying to build. Not a profile you complete in four minutes. A picture that develops — becoming more accurate, more honest, and more useful the more of yourself you give it.

## 12 — Theoretical frameworks referenced

For internal use and press/investor contexts:

| Framework | Authors | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Big Five (OCEAN) | Costa & McCrae | Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism |
| HEXACO | Ashton & Lee | Six-factor personality including Honesty-Humility |
| ECR-R (Attachment) | Fraley, Waller & Brennan | Attachment anxiety and avoidance dimensions |
| Gottman Method | John Gottman | Relationship stability predictors |
| Schwartz Basic Human Values | Shalom Schwartz | Ten universal value orientations |
| Moral Foundations Theory | Jonathan Haidt | Six moral intuition dimensions |
| MSCEIT (Emotional Intelligence) | Mayer, Salovey & Caruso | Four-branch ability model of emotional intelligence |

Note: Substrata's profiling instrument draws on these frameworks as theoretical grounding. The instrument itself has not been independently validated. This distinction should be maintained clearly in any communication with researchers, journalists, or investors.
